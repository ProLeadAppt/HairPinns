# Jena's public voice

This is the writing standard for Hair Pinns emails, articles, website copy and social captions.

## The voice in one sentence

Jena sounds like a friendly, experienced hairdresser speaking to a real client in her chair: warm, direct, practical, honest and lightly cheeky.

## What to keep

- Write in first person when the message comes from Jena.
- Use short sentences and everyday words.
- Give the useful answer early.
- Sound confident without pretending every product suits everyone.
- Use Australian English: colour, favourite, personalised and organised.
- Let a little humour through when it feels natural.
- Invite a reply when Jena can genuinely help someone choose.
- Use contractions such as "I'm", "you'll" and "it's".
- Keep product names, prices, availability and offer terms exactly as Shopify states them.

## What to remove

- No em dashes.
- No corporate filler or polished marketing speak.
- No phrases such as "elevate your routine", "unlock", "step into", "discover the difference", "seamless experience" or "thoughtfully curated".
- No fake urgency, invented scarcity or countdown language.
- No unsupported health, ingredient, performance or longevity claims.
- No long introductions before the useful point.
- No guilt in abandoned-cart or recovery messages.
- No pretending a mass email is a one-to-one note.

## Jena's natural patterns

Good openings:

- "Jena here. Quick one from me."
- "I know, Christmas has a habit of sneaking up on us."
- "If your roots are waving hello, it might be time."
- "Hair behaving badly between visits?"
- "No worries. I kept your bag for you."

Good light humour:

- "Or yours. I am not judging."
- "Christmas sorted. Well, the hair bit anyway."
- "Frizzy, flat, dry, wild, send it all."

Avoid humour in safety, complaint, refund or consent messages.

## Email structure

1. A short, specific subject.
2. Preview text that adds new information.
3. A direct opening from Jena.
4. One useful idea, offer or update.
5. One main action.
6. A genuine invitation to reply when relevant.

Use one primary button. Secondary destinations should be simple text links and only appear when they are genuinely useful.

## Blog structure

1. Answer the question in the first paragraph.
2. Explain what Jena sees behind the chair.
3. Give practical steps in plain language.
4. Separate personal experience from verified product facts.
5. Link to a relevant product, service or booking page only when it helps.
6. End with one clear next step.

## Fact and consent checks

Before publishing, confirm:

- Product details and availability against Shopify.
- Service details and pricing against the current booking system.
- Promotions and end dates with Jena.
- Health or performance claims against an approved source.
- Client image and story consent.
- Links, tracking parameters and the public web edition.

If a fact is not confirmed, remove it or ask Jena. Do not fill the gap with persuasive copy.
